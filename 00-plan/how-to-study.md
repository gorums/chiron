# How to study this without wasting your 30 hours

Ten minutes here protects the other 29 hours and 50 minutes.

## 1. Pick your one business now

Every exercise applies to a single real business. Choose one and write it at the top of your notes file. Good choices:

- A business idea you actually have
- Your employer
- A local business you know well (the gym you go to, a friend's restaurant)

Bad choice: "a generic SaaS company." Abstraction is where learning goes to die.

## 2. The 3-pass method per module

For each module, in one sitting:

1. **Read** the module straight through without stopping (about a third of the module's time budget).
2. **Do the exercise** for your chosen business (about half). This is the part that creates skill. Reading without doing produces the illusion of competence — you will feel like you understand marketing and be unable to do any.
3. **Write 5 lines from memory** into your notes, no peeking (the rest). What were the key ideas? Retrieval practice is roughly twice as effective per minute as re-reading.

## 3. Rules that matter

**Never read two modules in a row without doing the exercise.** The single biggest failure mode for self-taught marketers is consuming content and building nothing.

**Timebox hard.** If a module says 90 minutes, set a timer. Stop at 90 even if unfinished; note where you stopped. Perfectionism is how a 30-hour plan becomes a 200-hour plan you abandon at hour 40.

**Keep one notes file.** `my-notes.md` in this folder. One file, appended forever. Not per-module files you never reopen.

**Spaced repetition, cheap version.** At the start of each session, spend 3 minutes writing down what you remember from the *previous* session before opening anything. Painful, and it is most of why this will stick.

## 4. What to do when you disagree or don't understand

Marketing has more confidently-stated nonsense per square metre than almost any other field, because results are slow and attribution is murky, so bad ideas survive. When something in here feels wrong, write down why. Test it against your chosen business. Some of it *is* wrong for your context — the goal is judgment, not obedience.

## 5. Progress tracking

The app tracks this for you. Mark each module complete when you finish it, and write your one-line takeaway in the module's notes box. Seeing 19 modules fill in is a real motivator, and your takeaways become a personal cheat sheet you can reread in ten minutes.

Two numbers on the Stats page are worth watching more than the completion count: your **quiz accuracy**, and your **calibration** — how often you were "certain" and wrong. The second one is the useful one. Beliefs you hold confidently and incorrectly are the ones that cost money.

## 6. The failure mode to watch for

Around hour 8–12, once you know the vocabulary, you will feel much more competent than you are. This is the standard curve. The cure is Part 3 and the capstone, which will humble you productively. Push through it — this is where most self-learners stop and stay permanently mediocre.

# Business Brief

**Use with:** M01 — What marketing actually is
**Time to fill:** 25 minutes
**Refill:** whenever the business changes materially, or every 6 months

This is the sheet you fill before you do anything else. Every later template assumes this one exists and is honest.

Duplicate this file, rename it `business-brief-<business>.md`, and fill it in. Delete the example text as you go.

---

## 1. The one-sentence definition

> We sell **[what]** to **[whom]** instead of **[what they would otherwise do]**.

Write it as one sentence. If you need two, your business is either two businesses or you have not decided yet.

**Your answer:**

```
We sell ______________ to ______________ instead of ______________.
```

**Filled example:**

```
We sell a fixed-price monthly bookkeeping service to owner-operated trades
businesses in Spain doing 100k-500k EUR revenue, instead of them doing it in
a spreadsheet at 11pm on Sundays or paying a traditional gestoria who only
talks to them once a quarter.
```

Notice what the example does: it names a specific buyer, a revenue band, and *two* named alternatives, one of which is "do it yourself badly."

**Filled example (rows 1-3):**

| # | Alternative | Why a buyer picks it | Why they don't | How often we lose to it |
|---|-------------|----------------------|----------------|--------------------------|
| 1 | Do nothing, keep the spreadsheet | Free, they already know how, no decision required | It breaks at tax time and they lose a weekend | ~50% of lost deals |

**The most important row is row 1.** Most businesses lose more deals to inertia than to named competitors. If you cannot say why someone changes from doing nothing, you do not have a marketing problem, you have a proposition problem.

**Weakest P:** ______________

**Filled example of a good "weakest P" answer:**

```
Weakest P: Place (2/5). We are only buyable by booking a 45-minute call with
the founder, which happens 6 days out. Roughly 40% of people who book do not
show. There is no way to buy without talking to a human, and no self-serve
starter tier. Our promotion is generating demand our distribution cannot absorb.
```

That is a useful answer because it names a mechanism and a number. "Our promotion is weak" is not an answer.

## What good looks like

A finished brief is specific enough that a stranger could read it and correctly guess what your next three marketing decisions should be. Every claim has a number or a named example behind it. The alternatives section names "do nothing" as a real competitor with a real share of losses. The constraint is one sentence, mechanical, and uncomfortable to read.

## Common failure

Writing the brief as marketing copy instead of a diagnosis. If your 4 Ps section says everything is a 4 or a 5, and your constraint is "brand awareness," you have written an advertisement for yourself and learned nothing. The brief is only useful when it is unflattering. Force yourself to give at least one P a score of 2 or lower and to justify it with evidence.

# Marketing Plan — One Page

**Use with:** M06 — Writing a marketing strategy
**Time to fill:** 90 minutes if the earlier templates are done; a week if they are not
**Refill:** quarterly. Rewrite fully once a year.

A strategy is a diagnosis, a guiding policy, and a coherent set of actions. It is not a list of tactics with dates on them. If this document does not say what you are **not** doing and why, it is a to-do list wearing a suit.

**Keep it to one page.** If it does not fit, you have not decided.

---

## 1. Diagnosis

What is actually going on? The obstacle, in mechanical terms. One paragraph, maximum five sentences. Use numbers.

```

```

**Filled example:**

```
We acquire 14 customers a month, 11 of them from branded and high-intent
search, which is already saturated: impression share on our core terms is 78%
and CPCs rose 34% year on year while volume was flat. Nobody outside the ~900
monthly searchers knows the category of service exists, so there is no top of
funnel to feed capture. Retention is strong (7% annual churn, 3,400 EUR LTV),
so the constraint is not the product; it is that we are fishing in a pond we
have already emptied.
```

**Bad diagnosis to avoid:** "We need to grow revenue by 40% and increase brand awareness in our market." That is a target and a wish, not a diagnosis.

---

## 2. Guiding policy

The overall approach that responds to the diagnosis. One or two sentences. It should rule things out.

```

```

**Filled example:**

```
Spend the next four quarters converting saturated capture into a creation
engine: build recognition among trades owners before they have a tax problem,
using one channel where they already congregate, and accept flat paid-search
volume rather than bidding CPCs up to defend it.
```

A guiding policy is testable: someone should be able to bring you a proposal and you can say "no, that contradicts the policy."

Rules of thumb worth stating explicitly so you can argue with them:

- Keep a named line for **testing** (10-20%) that is allowed to fail without anyone being blamed.
- If creation is under 20% of spend and you are demand-constrained, the plan contradicts the diagnosis.
- Infrastructure (tracking, tooling, site work) is real spend. Hiding it makes channel CAC look better than it is.

## What good looks like

Someone who does not work with you can read this page and predict what you will say no to. The diagnosis contains at least three numbers. The excluded-channels table has a real reason and a real trigger for each row, not "no budget." Every 90-day action has a "done when" that a stranger could verify. The plan is internally consistent: the budget split matches the guiding policy, and the metrics match the objectives.

## Common failure

Writing a plan where every channel is included at low intensity. Doing eight channels badly is the single most common way small marketing teams waste a year: nothing gets enough budget to leave its learning phase, nothing gets enough hours to get good, and no result is clear enough to decide anything. A plan that funds two channels properly and names six it refuses to run is almost always the better document.

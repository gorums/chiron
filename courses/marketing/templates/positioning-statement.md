# Positioning Statement and Message Hierarchy

**Use with:** M03 — Positioning, brand, messaging
**Time to fill:** 60 minutes
**Refill:** annually, or when a new competitor changes what buyers compare you against

Positioning is not a slogan. It is a decision about **what you want to be compared to**, made deliberately instead of by accident. If you do not choose, the customer chooses for you, usually badly.

Work through parts 1-5 in order. Do not skip to the statement; the statement is the output, not the input.

---

## Part 1 — Competitive alternatives

What would the customer do if you did not exist? Not "who are our competitors" — competitors are a category you invented. Alternatives are what actually happens.

| # | Alternative | Category the customer puts it in | What it does well |
|---|-------------|----------------------------------|-------------------|
| 1 | Do nothing / status quo | | |
| 2 | | | |
| 3 | | | |
| 4 | | | |

**Filled example:**

| # | Alternative | Category | What it does well |
|---|-------------|----------|-------------------|
| 1 | Spreadsheet + do it yourself | "Being careful with money" | Free, total control, no trust required |
| 2 | Local gestoria | "An accountant" | Personal relationship, cheap, culturally default |
| 3 | Cloud accounting SaaS | "Software" | Cheap, modern, always available |
| 4 | Hire a part-time bookkeeper | "Staff" | Dedicated, on your terms |

Note that these sit in **four different categories**. That is normal and it is exactly the problem positioning solves: you must decide which of these comparisons you want to win.

What do you have that the alternatives do not? Facts only. Capabilities, assets, structural advantages. Not adjectives.

**Test:** could a competitor put the same words on their site tomorrow without lying? If yes, it is not an attribute, it is a platitude. "Great customer service," "innovative," "trusted," "passionate about our clients" all fail this test.

## Part 3 — Value they enable

For each attribute, what does the customer actually get? Push through two levels: attribute leads to a capability, capability leads to a life outcome.

| Attribute | So the customer can... | Which means... (the outcome they'd tell a friend) |
|-----------|------------------------|---------------------------------------------------|
| | | |
| | | |
| | | |
| | | |

**Filled example:**

| Attribute | So the customer can... | Which means... |
|-----------|------------------------|----------------|
| Only serve trades businesses | Skip explaining what a retention or a materials deposit is | "They already know how my business works" |
| Fixed published price | Ask a question without wondering if it costs 90 EUR | "I actually talk to my accountant now" |
| WhatsApp, 4-hour SLA | Get an answer from a van at 7am | "I don't have to be at a desk to run my finances" |
| One-page monthly summary | Know if the last month was good in 30 seconds | "I finally understand my own numbers" |

The third column is the copy. The first column is the proof.

---

## Part 4 — Who cares most

Of everyone who could buy, who cares *disproportionately* about the value in Part 3? Describe them by characteristic, not by name.

- **They care most when:** ______________
- **Segment characteristics that predict caring:** ______________
- **Who explicitly does not care (and that is fine):** ______________

**Filled example:**

- They care most when: revenue is growing past what a spreadsheet handles (roughly 150k EUR) and the owner has just been penalised or scared once.
- Predicts caring: owner-operated, 2-8 staff, no office manager, growing, has had one tax scare in the last 18 months.
- Does not care: sub-60k solo autonomos (price-only), and firms over 1M EUR (they want an in-house finance person and a real advisor).

If you cannot name a group that does not care, you have not positioned, you have described.

---

## Part 5 — Market category

The category is the context you put yourself in so the buyer knows what to compare you to and what to expect. Choosing wrong is expensive: a category that is too big means you get compared to giants; too new means you must fund the education of the whole market.

- **Category we claim:** ______________
- **What that makes the buyer assume (good):** ______________
- **What that makes the buyer assume (bad, and how we counter it):** ______________
- **Categories we deliberately reject and why:** ______________

**Filled example:**

- Category we claim: "Bookkeeping and tax service for trades businesses."
- Good assumptions: it is a service, there is a human, it is recurring, it replaces the gestoria.
- Bad assumptions: "so it's expensive and slow like a gestoria." Countered by the published fixed price and the 4-hour SLA on the first screen.
- Rejected: "accounting software" (invites price comparison with a 30 EUR tool and makes the human the cost, not the value). Rejected: "financial advisory" (implies advice we do not give and a price we do not charge).

## Part 6 — The assembled positioning statement

This is an internal document. It is not going on the website. Its job is to make the whole team make consistent decisions.

> For **[who cares most]**
> who **[situation / trigger]**,
> **[product name]** is a **[market category]**
> that **[key value delivered]**.
> Unlike **[primary competitive alternative]**,
> we **[the unique attribute that makes the value credible]**.

**Your statement:**

```
For ______________________________________________
who ______________________________________________,
______________ is a ______________________________
that _____________________________________________.
Unlike ___________________________________________,
we _______________________________________________.
```

**Filled example:**

```
For owner-operated trades businesses doing 150k-500k EUR
who have just been burned once by a filing they did themselves,
Cuentas Claras is a bookkeeping and tax service for trades businesses
that gives them a plain-Spanish picture of their money every month
without them touching a spreadsheet.
Unlike a traditional gestoria,
we work only with trades, publish a fixed monthly price, and answer on
WhatsApp within four working hours.
```

**Sanity checks before you accept it:**

- [ ] Could a competitor sign their name under this statement unchanged? If yes, rewrite.
- [ ] Does it name a specific alternative you are trying to beat?
- [ ] Would the customers you interviewed recognise themselves in line 1 and 2?
- [ ] Is there anything in it you cannot prove today?

## What good looks like

A stranger reading only Part 6 could tell you which of your four competitive alternatives you are attacking and what evidence you would show to win. Every pillar has at least one proof that is a number or a named customer. The statement excludes people on purpose, and you can name who it excludes without flinching.

## Common failure

Writing the positioning statement as a superlative sandwich — "the leading, most trusted, all-in-one platform for modern businesses." It contains no comparison, so it makes no decision, so it changes nothing about what you build or say. The second failure is positioning against a competitor the customer has never heard of, while the real alternative — doing nothing — goes unaddressed on every page you own.

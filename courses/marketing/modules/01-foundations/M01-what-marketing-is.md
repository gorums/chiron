# M01 — What Marketing Actually Is

**Time:** 60 minutes (25 read · 25 exercise · 10 recall)
**Part 1 of 6 · Foundations**

---

## Why this matters

Most people think marketing means advertising and promotion. That belief is the reason most marketing money is wasted. If you internalize the correct definition in the next 25 minutes, you will already be ahead of a meaningful share of people who do this professionally.

---

## Core concepts

### The definition worth memorizing

> Marketing is the work of understanding a group of people well enough to build, price, place, and describe something they will choose over the alternatives — profitably and repeatedly.

Four things to notice:

1. **Understanding comes first.** Research is not a nice-to-have that precedes the real work; it is the work.
2. **It includes the product itself.** What you build and what you charge are marketing decisions, not just engineering and finance ones.
3. **"Over the alternatives."** Marketing is always comparative. The alternative is often "do nothing," which is the toughest competitor you will ever face.
4. **Profitably and repeatedly.** Buying customers at a loss is not marketing. It is a subsidy.

Advertising is one tactic inside this. Roughly the last 10%.

### Demand capture vs. demand creation

This distinction organizes almost everything in digital marketing.

**Demand capture** — someone already wants the thing and is looking. You intercept them.
Channels: search ads, SEO, marketplaces, comparison sites, review sites.
Economics: high conversion rates, high cost per click, capped by how many people are searching. You cannot grow beyond existing demand.

**Demand creation** — nobody was looking. You make them aware of a problem or a possibility.
Channels: paid social, video, content, PR, influencers, events, brand campaigns.
Economics: low conversion rates, cheap attention, hard to measure, but it is the only way to expand your market.

Nearly every "our marketing stopped working" story is a company that only did capture, saturated the existing demand, and had built no creation engine to refill it.

### The 4 Ps (and why they still matter in 2026)

Product, Price, Place, Promotion. Devised in the 1960s, still correct, because they simply enumerate the levers you control. Most beginners obsess over Promotion, which is the weakest of the four.

- **Product** wrong → no promotion saves you.
- **Price** wrong → you either bleed margin or repel your best customers.
- **Place** (distribution — where it can be bought, how it gets there) wrong → nobody can act on the demand you generated. Historically, distribution beats promotion more often than not.
- **Promotion** → what everyone calls "marketing."

For services and software the extended 7 Ps add People, Process, Physical evidence (proof: reviews, case studies, certifications, a credible-looking website). For anything sold online, physical evidence is disproportionately important — trust is the actual conversion bottleneck.

### Marketing vs. sales vs. growth vs. brand

- **Brand** — what people believe about you before you speak. It is a memory structure, and it lowers the cost of everything else you do.
- **Marketing** — creating and capturing demand at a system level.
- **Growth** — usually means the experimental, data-heavy subset focused on the funnel and the product loop.
- **Sales** — converting individual identified humans, one at a time.

These fight over budget in every company. Understanding which is being argued for is a survival skill.

### The uncomfortable truth about how it works

Marketing effects are mostly **slow, cumulative, and hard to attribute**. A large share of the effect of an ad is on people who buy months later through a channel that gets the credit. This mismatch — real long-run effects, weak short-run measurability — is why marketing has so much confidently-stated nonsense in it, and why the measurement modules (M13, M15) matter more than the tactics modules.

## How it works in practice

A marketer's actual week looks roughly like:

- 20% talking to customers or reading what they wrote (support tickets, reviews, sales calls)
- 20% making things (copy, ads, pages, emails)
- 20% looking at numbers and deciding what to change
- 20% coordinating with sales, product, and finance
- 20% arguing about priorities

Not one minute of it is "coming up with creative ideas" in the way films depict. Ideas are the cheap part.

## 2026 reality check

Three shifts that make the fundamentals *more* valuable, not less:

1. **AI collapsed the cost of production.** Anyone can produce a thousand competent blog posts and a hundred ad variants. That means execution volume is no longer a moat. Judgment, positioning, and knowing what is worth saying are the moat.
2. **Distribution surfaces are consolidating into answer engines.** Roughly 60% of Google searches now end without a click to any website, and when an AI Overview appears, the click-through rate of the top organic result drops by more than half. Whole categories of "just rank and get traffic" strategies quietly died. Being *the brand the AI names* is the new fight.
3. **The ad platforms became black boxes.** Google's Performance Max and Meta's Advantage+ took targeting decisions away from marketers. Your inputs — offer, creative, customer data, and what you tell the algorithm to optimize toward — are now the job. Which means the fundamentals in Part 1 *are* the paid media skill now.

## Common mistakes

- **Confusing marketing with promotion.** Leads to fixing an ad when the problem is the price.
- **Copying tactics from companies in a different situation.** What works for a brand with existing awareness will not work for you. Always ask: what was true for them that isn't true for me?
- **Optimizing what is easy to measure.** Search ads are measurable and often not incremental (see M15) — people would have found you anyway. Brand-building is barely measurable and often decisive.
- **Treating "the market" as one thing.** There is no general audience. There are segments (M02).
- **Believing the funnel is orderly.** Real buying journeys loop, stall, and involve other people. The funnel is a useful accounting fiction, not a description of reality.

## Exercise (25 minutes)

Take out `templates/business-brief.md` and fill it in for **your chosen business**:

1. In one sentence, what is sold, to whom, and instead of what?
2. List the real alternatives your buyer considers — include "do nothing" and "hire someone / do it manually."
3. Classify your current marketing efforts (or a competitor's, if you have none) into **demand capture** vs **demand creation**. What is the split?
4. For each of the 4 Ps, write one sentence about your business. Which of the four is weakest? Be honest — that is where the leverage is.
5. Write down: what would have to be true for this business to double? Name it as a constraint, not a wish. ("We have no way to reach owners of X" is a constraint. "More sales" is a wish.)

## If you remember one thing

**Marketing is the whole system of understanding, building, pricing, distributing, and describing an offer — not the promotion at the end.** When something is not working, check the offer before you check the ad.

# Glossary

Every term you will hear in a marketing meeting, in one line of plain language.

**Terms marked with a leading asterisk (\*) are the ones to memorise first.** There are about 45 of them. Knowing those cold will let you follow any marketing conversation and will make you sound like you have done this before. The rest you can look up when they come up.

If a term below is used at you in a way that does not match this definition, ask what they mean by it. Half of all marketing disagreements are two people using the same word for different things.

---

## A

**\*A/B test** — Splitting traffic between two versions to see which performs better; only meaningful if you have enough volume to detect a real difference.

**Above the fold** — What is visible before scrolling. Less sacred than it used to be, but still where clarity has to happen.

**Account-based marketing (ABM)** — B2B approach that targets a named list of companies rather than a broad audience, treating each account as a market of one.

**Ad rank** — Google's internal score deciding whether and where your ad shows; roughly bid times quality times expected impact of extras.

**\*Advantage+** — Meta's automated campaign products that take most targeting and placement decisions away from you and hand them to the algorithm; your inputs become creative, offer, and data.

**\*AEO (Answer Engine Optimization)** — Optimising so that AI assistants and answer boxes give your answer and name you, rather than optimising for a blue link.

**Affiliate marketing** — Paying third parties a commission per sale they refer; cheap-looking but frequently pays for demand you already had.

**\*AOV (Average Order Value)** — Revenue divided by number of orders. Raising it is often easier than getting more orders.

**Assisted conversion** — A touchpoint that appeared somewhere in the path but was not the last click; useful directionally, dangerous if you add them up.

**\*Attribution** — Assigning credit for a conversion to marketing touchpoints. It is a modelling choice, not a measurement, and every model is wrong in a different direction.

**\*Attribution window** — How long after seeing or clicking an ad a conversion still gets credited (e.g. 7-day click, 1-day view). Changing the window changes every number in your report, so always state it.

**Audience exclusion** — Deliberately removing people from targeting (existing customers, recent converters, bad-fit segments). Underused and free.

**Awareness** — Whether people know you exist. Cheap to buy, slow to build, and the precondition for everything downstream.

---

## B

**Backlink** — A link from another site to yours. Still a meaningful ranking factor; still mostly obtained by having something worth linking to.

**Bid strategy** — The rule an ad platform uses to set your bids (manual CPC, target CPA, target ROAS, maximise conversions). Choosing the wrong one is a common way to waste a month.

**Blended CAC** — Total marketing and sales spend divided by all new customers, including organic ones. Flatters paid channels; useful as a business-level number, misleading as a channel-level one.

**Bottom of funnel (BOFU)** — Content or campaigns aimed at people close to deciding: pricing, comparisons, demos, reviews.

**Bounce rate** — Share of visits with no further interaction. Redefined in GA4 as the inverse of engaged sessions, so old benchmarks do not transfer.

**\*Brand** — What people believe about you before you say anything. A memory structure that lowers the cost of every other marketing activity.

**Brand lift study** — A survey-based test measuring whether ads changed awareness or perception. Offered free by large platforms above certain spend levels.

**\*Branded search** — People searching your name. The most reliable free indicator that demand creation is working; watch it monthly.

**Broad match** — Google Ads match type that shows your ad on loosely related queries. Powerful with good conversion data, a money incinerator without it.

**Buying committee** — Everyone involved in a B2B purchase (typically 6-10 people). In B2C the equivalent is a spouse or a group chat, and it is just as real.

---

## C

**\*CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)** — Total acquisition spend divided by new customers acquired. Include people costs or you are lying to yourself.

**\*CAC payback** — Months of contribution margin needed to earn back CAC. The number that decides whether you can afford to grow.

**\*Canonical tag** — HTML tag telling search engines which URL is the real version of a page. Prevents duplicate-content problems, especially from URL parameters.

**Category entry point** — The situation or cue that makes someone think of a category ("the boiler broke", "we just hired our tenth person"). You want to be the brand attached to as many of these as possible.

**\*Churn** — The rate at which customers leave. Small changes here move LTV more than almost anything you can do in an ad account.

**\*Click-through rate (CTR)** — Clicks divided by impressions. Diagnostic of relevance; easy to optimise in ways that hurt revenue.

**\*Cohort** — A group defined by when they joined, tracked over time. The only honest way to see whether retention is improving.

**Content-market fit** — When your content reaches and resonates with the people who could actually buy from you, rather than with a general audience or with other marketers.

**\*Contribution margin** — Revenue minus variable costs. The money actually available to pay for acquisition. Use it, never revenue, when calculating LTV.

**\*Conversion rate (CVR)** — Share of visitors or leads who take the target action. Always state which two steps you mean.

**\*Core Web Vitals** — Google's page experience metrics: LCP (loading, target under 2.5s), INP (responsiveness, under 200ms), CLS (visual stability, under 0.1).

**\*CPA (Cost Per Acquisition/Action)** — Spend divided by actions. Meaningless until you say which action.

**\*CPC (Cost Per Click)** — What you pay per click. Set by auction competition, not by you.

**CPL (Cost Per Lead)** — Spend divided by leads. Falls easily by lowering lead quality, so never report it without a downstream metric.

**\*CPM (Cost Per Mille)** — Cost per thousand impressions. The currency of awareness buying, and a useful proxy for how competitive an audience is.

**Creative fatigue** — Performance decay as an audience sees the same ad repeatedly. Shows up first as rising CPMs and falling CTR at flat spend.

**CRM** — The system holding your customer and deal records. Whatever it says is what the business will believe, so it is a marketing responsibility whether you like it or not.

**\*CRO (Conversion Rate Optimization)** — Improving the share of visitors who act. Mostly clarity, message match, proof, and removing friction; only occasionally button colours.

**Customer journey** — The real path from unaware to purchase. Loops, stalls, involves other people, and never looks like the funnel diagram.

---

## D

**Dark funnel** — Influence that happens where you cannot see it: WhatsApp groups, podcasts, private Slack, DMs. Real, large, and permanently unattributable.

**Dayparting** — Scheduling ads for particular hours or days. Usually a small lever; occasionally decisive for businesses that answer the phone.

**\*Demand capture** — Reaching people who already want the thing and are looking. High conversion, high cost, capped by existing demand.

**\*Demand creation** — Making people aware of a problem or possibility they were not thinking about. Low conversion, cheap attention, the only way to grow the market.

**Deliverability** — Whether your email reaches the inbox rather than spam. Governed by authentication, sending reputation, engagement, and complaint rates.

**\*DKIM** — Email authentication that cryptographically signs your messages so receiving servers can verify they were not altered and came from you.

**\*DMARC** — Email policy record that tells receiving servers what to do with mail failing SPF and DKIM, and sends you reports. Required by major mailbox providers for bulk senders.

**Double opt-in** — Requiring a confirmation click before adding someone to a list. Smaller list, dramatically better deliverability and engagement.

**Dynamic search ads** — Google Ads that generate headlines and targeting from your website content. Good for coverage, poor for control.

---

## E

**\*E-E-A-T** — Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness. Google's quality framework. The first E — demonstrable first-hand experience — is the hardest to fake and the most valuable in an era of generated content.

**Engagement rate** — Interactions divided by reach or impressions. Definition varies by platform; almost always a vanity metric unless tied to an outcome.

**Enhanced conversions** — Sending hashed first-party data (email, phone) back to ad platforms to improve conversion measurement after cookie loss.

**Entity (SEO)** — A thing search engines recognise as distinct — a person, brand, product, place. Being a recognised entity is increasingly what gets you named in AI answers.

**\*Event (GA4)** — GA4's unit of measurement. Everything is an event; some events are marked as key events (the old "goals").

**Exact match** — Match type showing your ad only for the specified query and close variants. Control at the cost of volume.

**Exit rate** — Share of sessions that end on a given page. Useful for finding the leak in a multi-step flow.

---

## F

**\*First-party data** — Data you collected directly from your own customers. The most durable asset in marketing as third-party tracking degrades.

**Flywheel** — A loop where each turn makes the next one easier (customers bring referrals, content brings links). The alternative to renting growth from ad platforms.

**\*Frequency** — Average number of times a person saw your ad. Too low and nothing lands; too high and you are annoying people you already convinced.

**\*Funnel** — The model of awareness to purchase. A useful accounting fiction for spotting where people drop out, not a description of how anyone actually buys.

---

## G

**\*GA4 (Google Analytics 4)** — The current Google Analytics: event-based, session-secondary, different from Universal Analytics in ways that break every old benchmark.

**\*GEO (Generative Engine Optimization)** — Making your brand the one AI models cite and recommend. Overlaps heavily with AEO; the industry has not settled on which term wins.

**Gross margin** — Revenue minus cost of goods sold, as a percentage. Sets the ceiling on what you can ever spend to acquire a customer.

**Guardrail metric** — A metric that must not get worse while you optimise something else. The thing that stops a conversion-rate win from destroying lead quality.

---

## H

**Hard bounce** — A permanently failed email delivery. Remove these immediately; leaving them in wrecks your sending reputation.

**\*Holdout** — A randomly chosen group deliberately excluded from marketing so you can compare them to the treated group. The cleanest way to know if anything you do is incremental.

**Hreflang** — Tag telling search engines which language and region version of a page to serve. Relevant the moment you sell in more than one country.

---

## I

**\*ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)** — A description of the situation in which someone buys from you, including who to disqualify. Not a persona with a stock photo.

**Impression** — One display of your ad or listing. Cheap, abundant, and only meaningful in ratios.

**Impression share** — Share of available impressions you received. Tells you how much room is left in a channel before you have to bid up.

**\*Incrementality** — Whether an activity caused sales that would not have happened otherwise. The only honest question in measurement, and answerable only through experiments, not dashboards.

**Index (search)** — Whether search engines have stored your page at all. Check in Search Console before diagnosing anything else about rankings.

**\*Intent** — What the person is actually trying to do. Usually classed as informational, commercial investigation, transactional, or navigational.

**Internal link** — A link from one page of your site to another. The cheapest SEO lever there is, and consistently neglected.

**IP warming** — Gradually increasing email volume from a new sending IP or domain so mailbox providers learn to trust it.

---

## J

**\*JTBD (Jobs To Be Done)** — The framing that people "hire" products to make progress in a situation. Written as: when [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [outcome].

---

## K

**\*Key event (GA4)** — GA4's name for a conversion: an event you have flagged as one that matters. Renamed from "conversion" to reduce confusion with ad platform conversions, which count differently.

**Keyword** — A query you want to appear for. Increasingly a planning abstraction rather than a literal targeting unit.

**Keyword cannibalization** — Multiple pages of yours competing for the same query, so none of them wins. Fix by consolidating, not by writing more.

**KPI (Key Performance Indicator)** — A metric someone has decided to be judged on. Choose few, define them exactly, and expect people to optimise whatever you pick.

---

## L

**Landing page** — The page traffic arrives on. Its job is to continue the sentence the ad started.

**\*Last-click attribution** — Giving all credit to the final touchpoint. Simple, universal, and systematically over-credits demand capture while under-crediting everything that created the demand.

**\*Learning phase** — The period after launching or editing a campaign while the platform gathers conversion data. Performance is unstable and unrepresentative; editing resets it, which is why the do-not-touch window exists.

**Lead magnet** — Something of value given in exchange for contact details. Works when it solves a real problem on its own, fails when it is a brochure with a form.

**Lead scoring** — Ranking leads by fit and behaviour so sales works the best ones first. Worth doing once you have enough volume to be wrong about priorities.

**Lift** — The difference between treated and untreated groups. The measurement that matters, as opposed to the raw number.

**List hygiene** — Regularly removing inactive and bouncing subscribers. Shrinks the list, raises revenue.

**\*Lookalike audience** — An audience the platform builds to resemble a source list you provide. Quality depends entirely on the quality of the seed list.

**\*LTV (Lifetime Value)** — Total contribution margin from a customer over a stated horizon. Use contribution, not revenue, and cap the horizon at something you have actually observed.

**\*LTV:CAC** — Ratio of lifetime value to acquisition cost. Around 3:1 is healthy; well above it usually means you are underinvesting, not winning.

---

## M

**\*MMM (Marketing Mix Modeling)** — Statistical modelling of how spend across channels relates to sales, using aggregate data. Privacy-durable, needs years of data, has come back into fashion as tracking degraded.

**\*MQL (Marketing Qualified Lead)** — A lead marketing believes is worth sales' time. The definition is negotiated between teams, and the negotiation matters more than the acronym.

**Media mix** — How budget is distributed across channels. The senior marketer's actual job.

**\*MER (Marketing Efficiency Ratio)** — Total revenue divided by total marketing spend. Blunt, un-gameable, and a useful sanity check when channel-level ROAS numbers add up to more revenue than the company made.

**MDE (Minimum Detectable Effect)** — The smallest change a test can reliably detect at your traffic level. Halving it roughly quadruples the sample you need.

**\*Mental availability** — How readily your brand comes to mind in a buying situation. Built by consistent, distinctive, broad reach over time.

**Modeled conversions** — Conversions the platform estimates rather than observes, filling gaps left by consent and tracking loss. Increasingly a large share of reported numbers.

**Multi-touch attribution** — Distributing credit across touchpoints by rule or model. More sophisticated-looking than last click and still not causal.

---

## N

**\*Negative keyword** — A query you block your ads from showing on. The single highest-return maintenance task in a Google Ads account.

**NPS (Net Promoter Score)** — Survey score from "how likely are you to recommend us". Weak as a metric, useful for the free-text comment that follows.

**NRR (Net Revenue Retention)** — Revenue from existing customers this period versus last, including expansion and churn. Above 100% means you grow without acquiring anyone.

**Noindex** — Instruction telling search engines not to include a page in results. Use on thin, duplicate, or paid-only pages.

---

## O

**\*Offer** — What someone gets, what they must give, and why now. Changing the offer beats changing the ad, reliably and repeatedly.

**Organic search** — Unpaid search traffic. Increasingly reduced by AI answers, and increasingly about being cited rather than clicked.

**\*Owned, earned, paid media** — Channels you control (site, list), coverage you earn (press, word of mouth), and attention you rent (ads). Owned compounds; paid stops the day you stop paying.

---

## P

**P-value** — Probability of seeing a result this extreme if there were no real difference. Below 0.05 is the usual bar; it is not the probability that you are right.

**Paid search** — Ads on search results. The purest demand capture channel, and often the least incremental.

**\*Payback period** — See CAC payback. The cash constraint that usually binds before the profitability constraint does.

**\*Performance Max (PMax)** — Google's automated campaign type running across all its inventory with minimal manual control. Your levers are budget, feed quality, creative assets, conversion goals, and exclusions.

**Personalization** — Varying content by segment or individual. Usually delivers less than promised and costs more than expected; do the segment level first.

**\*Physical availability** — How easy you are to buy: distribution, shelf space, listings, payment methods, opening hours. Historically beats promotion more often than not.

**Pipeline** — Total value of open deals. The B2B marketing team's real output, since revenue arrives quarters later.

**\*Positioning** — The deliberate choice of what you want to be compared to, and why you win that comparison. Always relative to alternatives, including doing nothing.

**Post-click / post-impression** — Conversions after a click versus after a view. View-through numbers are far softer and should never be added to click-through numbers without saying so.

**Programmatic** — Automated buying of display and video inventory through exchanges. Efficient at scale, an excellent way to waste a small budget invisibly.

**Proof** — Evidence a claim is true. Ranked by power: the customer's own numbers, third-party data, named quotes, demonstration, your own claim.

---

## Q

**Quality Score** — Google's 1-10 diagnostic of ad relevance, expected CTR, and landing page experience. Not a lever itself; a readout of things you should fix.

**Query** — What someone actually typed. Different from a keyword, which is what you targeted.

---

## R

**Reach** — Number of unique people who saw something. The denominator of frequency.

**\*Regression to the mean** — Extreme results tend to be followed by more average ones. The reason your "winning" week was probably partly luck, and why interventions after a bad month look effective.

**\*Retargeting (remarketing)** — Advertising to people who already visited. Cheap and impressive-looking on last-click reports; often the least incremental spend in the account, because many would have returned anyway.

**\*Retention** — Keeping customers. It multiplies the value of every acquisition euro you will ever spend, and it is where the leverage usually is.

**Rich results** — Enhanced search listings (stars, FAQs, prices) driven by structured data.

**\*ROAS (Return On Ad Spend)** — Revenue divided by ad spend. Platform-reported ROAS is measured by the platform grading its own homework; treat it as directional.

**ROI (Return On Investment)** — Profit relative to cost. Distinct from ROAS because it accounts for margin and all other costs, which is why it is usually much less flattering.

---

## S

**\*SQL (Sales Qualified Lead)** — A lead sales has accepted as worth pursuing. The MQL-to-SQL rate is the honest measure of whether marketing's leads are real.

**\*Schema markup** — Structured data in your HTML that tells machines what a page is about. Increasingly relevant to being parsed and cited by AI systems.

**Search intent** — See Intent.

**\*Segment** — A group of buyers who share a situation and behave similarly. There is no "general audience"; there are only segments.

**SERP** — The search engine results page. Look at the actual SERP before targeting a query; what is on it decides whether ranking is even worth anything.

**Share of search** — Your brand's share of category-related searches. A cheap, surprisingly predictive proxy for market share.

**\*SOV (Share of Voice)** — Your share of category advertising. The classic rule of thumb: hold SOV above your market share to grow, below it to shrink.

**Signal loss** — The ongoing degradation of tracking from consent rules, browser restrictions, and app privacy changes. The reason experiments and MMM came back.

**Single-minded proposition** — The one thing a campaign says. If there are two, there is no proposition.

**Sitemap** — A file listing your URLs for search engines. Basic hygiene, submitted in Search Console.

**\*SPF** — DNS record listing which servers may send email for your domain. One third of the SPF/DKIM/DMARC trio required to reach inboxes reliably.

**\*Statistical significance** — Confidence that a difference is not noise. Necessary but not sufficient: a significant result on a trivial effect is still worthless.

**\*Survivorship bias** — Studying only the winners. It is why case studies, "how we grew to 10k customers" posts, and most marketing advice generalise badly — the identical strategy that failed for a hundred others was never written up.

---

## T

**Test-and-learn** — Running deliberate experiments rather than arguing from opinion. Requires pre-committed criteria to be anything other than a slogan.

**Time to value** — How long before a new customer gets the thing they bought it for. Shortening it is usually the highest-return retention work available.

**Top of funnel (TOFU)** — Activity aimed at people who do not yet know they have a problem.

**\*Trigger** — The event that starts someone looking for a solution. Find it, and you know when and where to be present.

**Trust signals** — Reviews, guarantees, certifications, real photos, a physical address. For anything sold online, trust is usually the actual conversion bottleneck.

---

## U

**\*Unit economics** — The profit and loss of a single customer or order. If they do not work, no amount of marketing volume fixes it; it just loses money faster.

**USP (Unique Selling Proposition)** — The one claim only you can make. Frequently asserted, rarely true; test it against what a competitor could say tomorrow.

**Upsell / cross-sell** — Selling more to an existing customer. Cheaper than acquisition by roughly an order of magnitude, and consistently under-resourced.

**\*UTM parameters** — Tags appended to URLs (source, medium, campaign, content, term) so analytics can identify where traffic came from. Agree a naming convention on day one and enforce it, or your data becomes unreadable within a quarter.

---

## V

**\*Value proposition** — What the customer gets, for whom, and why it beats the alternative. Distinct from a slogan, which is how it is said.

**\*Vanity metric** — A number that rises reliably and changes no decision: impressions, followers, reach, engagement rate in isolation.

**\*View-through conversion** — A conversion credited to someone who saw but did not click an ad. The softest currency in advertising; never combine with click conversions in one total.

**Viewability** — Whether an ad was actually visible on screen. A large share of bought impressions never are.

---

## W

**Win rate** — Share of opportunities that close. A marketing metric as much as a sales one, because lead quality drives it.

**Word of mouth** — The most effective and least controllable channel. Mostly earned through product and time-to-value, not through campaigns.

---

## Z

**\*Zero-click search** — A search that ends without a click to any site, because the answer appeared on the results page. Now roughly 60% of Google searches, and the central strategic fact of SEO in 2026.

---

## The 45 to memorise first

If you learn nothing else, learn these, grouped by what they let you do.

**Do the arithmetic:** CAC, LTV, LTV:CAC, contribution margin, CAC payback, unit economics, AOV, CPM, CPC, CPA, CTR, CVR, ROAS, MER, churn, cohort.

**Think strategically:** demand capture, demand creation, positioning, ICP, JTBD, segment, offer, trigger, brand, mental availability, physical availability, SOV, owned/earned/paid, value proposition.

**Not be fooled:** attribution, attribution window, last-click, incrementality, holdout, MMM, survivorship bias, regression to the mean, statistical significance, vanity metric, view-through conversion, retargeting, learning phase.

**Operate the channels:** GA4 key event, UTM parameters, negative keyword, PMax, Advantage+, lookalike, Core Web Vitals, canonical, schema, E-E-A-T, AEO/GEO, zero-click search, SPF/DKIM/DMARC, first-party data, retention.

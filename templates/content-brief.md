# Content Brief

**Use with:** M08 (SEO and AI search) and M09 (content and organic social)
**Time to fill:** 30 minutes
**Rule:** no piece gets written without one. The brief is where the thinking happens; the writing is execution.

In 2026 a competent draft costs almost nothing to produce. That means the draft is not the valuable part. The valuable part is deciding what is worth saying, and having something to say that nobody else can say. This brief is built around that constraint.

**Filled example:**

| Field | Answer |
|-------|--------|
| Primary query | "how much does a gestoria cost for an autonomo" |
| Volume | 1,300/mo |
| Secondary | "precio gestoria autonomo 2026", "cuanto cobra una gestoria", "gestoria online precio" |
| Intent | Commercial investigation — they are pricing an alternative, not learning a concept |
| What ranks | Two comparison-site listicles with no real numbers, one competitor's pricing page |
| AI Overview | Yes. It currently gives a vague 40-100 EUR/month range with no conditions attached. |
| Realistic outcome | Being the source with actual ranges by business type is the opening. Cite-ability matters more than position 1 here. |

**Reality check before proceeding:** roughly 60% of searches now end without a click, and when an AI Overview is present the top organic result loses more than half its click-through. If the only value of this piece is traffic from this query, and an AI Overview already answers it well, the piece may not be worth writing. Say so and pick something else. This is the most useful decision this brief can produce.

**Why is this piece worth writing anyway?**

- [ ] The AI answer is currently wrong or vague and we can be the correction
- [ ] It serves people who will click because they need to evaluate *us*
- [ ] It is not for search at all — it is for sales enablement, email, or social
- [ ] It builds the entity/topic authority we need for a cluster
- [ ] It is a genuine differentiator that will get referenced by others

## 3. Angle

The point of view. What this piece argues that the existing results do not.

```
Angle:
```

**Filled example:** "Price is the wrong question. The variable that decides your cost is how much of the bookkeeping arrives already organised, and nobody tells you that, so here are real ranges by how messy your inputs are."

**Test:** could the top-ranking article have this angle? If yes, you are writing the eleventh version of an existing page and you will lose on domain authority alone.

**Filled example:**

| Type | What we have | Use |
|------|--------------|-----|
| Proprietary data | Actual monthly fees paid by 340 trades clients before they moved to us, by revenue band | The core table of the article — real ranges, n stated |
| Direct experience | We have done catch-up work on 60 messy accounts and know the hours it takes | The "what makes it expensive" section, with hours |
| Access to people | Two gestores who will speak candidly off the record | One quote each on how pricing is really set |
| Contrarian position | Cheap monthly fees are usually repriced upward once the mess is visible | The argument the listicles will never make |
| Something we built | A 6-field cost estimator | Embedded, and the reason people link to this page |

**If this box is empty, do not write the piece.** A brief with an empty "what only we can say" is a request for generic content, and generic content in 2026 is a rounding error: nobody links to it, no model cites it, and it does not move anybody toward buying. Go and get an asset — run an analysis on your own data, interview two customers, or build a small tool — and come back.

## 5. Outline

H2/H3 level. Answer the primary question in the first 100 words; do not make people scroll for it.

```
H1:

Opening (answer the question immediately, in 2-3 sentences):

H2:
  - point
  - point
H2:
  - point
  - point
H2:
  - point
H2:
  - point
Close / next step:
```

**Structural rules:**

- Answer first, then justify. The inverted pyramid works for both humans who are scanning and for models that extract.
- One idea per H2, and write H2s as the questions people actually ask.
- Tables and lists for anything comparative — they are easier for a human to scan and far more likely to be extracted as an answer.
- No throat-clearing. Delete every sentence before the answer that begins "In today's fast-paced business environment."

---

## 6. Structured answer block (for AI citation)

Include a self-contained block that answers the primary question completely in 40-80 words, near the top, without pronouns that depend on earlier context and without "as we mentioned above." This is what gets lifted into an answer engine, and it needs to make sense standing alone.

```
Question:

Answer (40-80 words, self-contained, includes the specific numbers or conditions):

```

**Filled example:**

```
Question: How much does a gestoria cost for a self-employed worker in Spain?

Answer: A gestoria for a self-employed worker in Spain typically costs 40-90
EUR per month for basic quarterly filings, and 90-180 EUR per month if you
invoice more than about 15 times a month or need payroll. In 2026, prices rose
roughly 8% year on year. The main cost driver is not your revenue but how
organised your records arrive; unsorted receipts typically add 30-60 EUR per
month.
```

**Checklist for the block:**

- [ ] Answers the question in the first sentence
- [ ] Contains specific numbers, ranges, or conditions
- [ ] Makes sense with zero surrounding context
- [ ] Names the entity (brand, place, year) so the citation is attributable
- [ ] Stated as fact, dated, and true — a model that cites you and is wrong is worse than not being cited

**Supporting technical items:**

- [ ] FAQ section with 3-6 real questions, each answered in under 60 words
- [ ] Appropriate schema markup (Article, FAQPage, HowTo, Product — pick honestly)
- [ ] Author byline with real credentials and a real bio page (E-E-A-T: the experience part is the part most sites fake and it is the part that matters)
- [ ] Date of publication and last update, visible
- [ ] Sources cited with links where claims are external
- [ ] Canonical tag correct; the page is not a near-duplicate of an existing one

|------------------------|---------|

| AI answer presence | Whether you are named/cited in answers to the target question, checked manually monthly across two or three assistants |

| Demand creation | Branded search volume and direct traffic, over quarters, not weeks |
| Link acquisition | Referring domains gained in 90 days |

Note that only the first of those is measurable in the first month, which is why most content programmes are judged on the wrong thing and killed too early.

## What good looks like

The "what only we can say" section contains an asset that took real effort to obtain — original data, a built tool, or genuine first-hand experience. The structured answer block is accurate, dated, and would stand alone if a model lifted it verbatim. The distribution plan names five real people. The success metric matches the actual purpose of the piece, and the check date is realistic rather than optimistic.

## Common failure

Writing the eleventh version of a page that already exists, at slightly better quality, and calling it a content strategy. Production is no longer the bottleneck; distinctiveness and distribution are. The second failure is judging a demand-creation piece by first-month traffic and killing it in week six, which is how organisations end up with an archive full of things that would have compounded had anyone waited.

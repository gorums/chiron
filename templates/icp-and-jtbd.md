# ICP and Jobs-To-Be-Done

**Use with:** M02 — Customers: segments, ICP, jobs-to-be-done
**Time to fill:** 45 minutes for the definition, plus 5 interviews of ~30 minutes each
**Refill:** after every 5 new interviews, or when close rates shift

An ICP is not a demographic sketch of a person. It is a description of the *situation* in which someone buys. "Marketing Mary, 34, drinks oat milk lattes" is a persona, and personas mostly produce bad copy. Situations produce good copy.

---

## Part 2 — Jobs-to-be-done statement

The format:

> **When** [situation], **I want to** [motivation / what they are trying to do], **so I can** [expected outcome].

Rules that make this work:

- The "when" is a situation with a time and a place, not a demographic.
- The "I want to" is in the customer's language and does not mention your product.
- The "so I can" is the outcome they would describe to a friend, which is usually emotional or social, not functional.

**Write three.** One functional, one emotional, one social. The one that makes you slightly uncomfortable is usually the true one.

```
1. When ______________________________________,
   I want to ____________________________________,
   so I can _____________________________________.

2. When ______________________________________,
   I want to ____________________________________,
   so I can _____________________________________.

3. When ______________________________________,
   I want to ____________________________________,
   so I can _____________________________________.
```

**Filled examples:**

```
Functional:
When the quarterly filing deadline is two weeks out and my invoices are in
three different places,
I want to hand the whole mess to someone who will not judge me,
so I can stop losing a Sunday every quarter.

Emotional:
When my accountant sends me a document I don't understand and I have to
pretend I do,
I want to be spoken to like a competent adult who just isn't an accountant,
so I can stop feeling stupid about my own business.

Social:
When another owner in the trade group mentions their books are handled,
I want to be the kind of business that has this sorted,
so I can look like I'm running a real company, not a van with a phone.
```

The emotional and social statements are where the ad copy comes from. The functional one is where the landing page headline comes from.

## Part 3 — The four forces

Every switch is a tug of war between four forces. Two push toward the new thing, two hold the customer in place. You need all four written down before you write any copy.

| Force | What it is | Your answer |
|-------|-----------|-------------|
| **Push** (of the current situation) | What is making the present intolerable | |
| **Pull** (of the new solution) | What is attractive about the new way | |
| **Anxiety** (about the new solution) | What could go wrong if they switch | |
| **Habit** (of the present) | What is comfortable about staying, including sunk cost and identity | |

**Filled example:**

| Force | Answer |
|-------|--------|
| Push | Lost a Sunday to reconciliation three quarters in a row; got a 200 EUR late-filing penalty in April |
| Pull | "Never think about this again"; a monthly one-page summary they actually understand |
| Anxiety | "Will they need me to organise the receipts anyway?"; "What if they miss something and I'm liable?"; "Am I locked in?" |
| Habit | The spreadsheet is *theirs*; they know where everything is; handing it over means admitting they were doing it badly; their spouse currently does it |

**The rule:** Most marketing only addresses Pull. Pull is the least effective of the four. Push and Anxiety usually decide the sale. Your best copy names the push in the customer's words and dissolves the top anxiety before it is asked.

**Now write:**

- Our headline should amplify this push: ______________
- Our page must defuse this anxiety in the first screen: ______________
- The habit we are competing with is: ______________

## Part 4 — Customer interview script

Ten questions. Every one is about **past behaviour**, not hypotheticals. People are unreliable narrators of their future and reliable narrators of their last Tuesday.

Rules before you start:

- Never pitch. If you pitch, the interview is over and you got nothing.
- Never ask "would you buy" or "how much would you pay." Both answers are worthless.
- Ask about the last time, not about times in general.
- After every answer, say "tell me more about that" at least once. The second answer is the true one.
- Shut up. Aim to talk less than 20% of the time.
- Record if they consent; otherwise take notes in their words, verbatim, not paraphrased.

### The script

1. **Walk me through the last time you dealt with [the problem]. Start from the morning it happened.**
   (Timeline, not summary. Keep them in the specific instance.)

2. **What was going on in the business that week that made it come up then, and not a month earlier?**
   (This is the trigger question. It is the highest-value question on the sheet.)

3. **What did you do first?**
   (Reveals where they actually look: WhatsApp group, Google, a friend, nothing.)

4. **Who else did you talk to about it?**
   (Buying committee, even in B2C. The spouse is a buying committee.)

5. **What else did you look at or consider? Including doing nothing.**
   (Real competitive set. Almost never the set you assumed.)

6. **What made you rule those out?**
   (Disqualifying criteria in their language. Becomes your comparison copy.)

7. **What almost stopped you from going ahead?**
   (Anxiety, unfiltered. Write this down word for word.)

8. **What did you expect to happen after you bought, and what actually happened?**
   (Gap between promise and delivery. Where churn and referrals are made.)

9. **Show me how you do it now — can you walk me through the actual steps or share your screen?**
   (Observed behaviour beats reported behaviour every time.)

10. **Who else do you know with this same problem, and what would you tell them?**
    (Referral mechanics, plus the natural-language description of your product that you should probably be using in your ads.)

**Closing:** "Is there anything I should have asked and didn't?" Then ask for one introduction.

## What good looks like

The disqualifier list is real and it costs you money to honour. The JTBD statements contain no product words. The four forces sheet has more written under Anxiety and Habit than under Pull. The interview log contains at least three verbatim quotes you would put on a landing page unedited, and at least one entry in the "Surprise" field that made you change the ICP.

## Common failure

Filling this in from imagination and calling it research. The second failure mode is interviewing people who are not in the ICP because they were easy to reach — friends, other marketers, people in your network — and then building a business for them. Log the "fits ICP?" field honestly; two useful interviews beat five polite ones.

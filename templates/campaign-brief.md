# Campaign Brief

**Use with:** M09-M11 (content, paid search, paid social), M19 capstone
**Time to fill:** 45 minutes
**Rule:** no campaign launches without one of these signed, including the success and kill criteria.

A campaign brief exists to force the arguments to happen *before* the money is spent. Half the value of this document is the fights it causes in the first 45 minutes.

One brief per campaign. If you cannot fit it on two pages, it is two campaigns.

## 1. Objective

One objective. Not three. State it as a number and a date.

```
Objective:
```

**Also state which stage of demand this serves:**

- [ ] Demand creation (people who were not looking)
- [ ] Demand capture (people already searching)
- [ ] Conversion / reactivation (people who already know us)

**Filled example:** "Acquire 60 new trial signups from trades-business owners in Andalusia between 1 Oct and 30 Nov at a cost per signup under 45 EUR. This is demand creation: the audience is not searching for us."

**Failing example:** "Increase brand awareness and drive engagement while generating qualified leads." That is three objectives and no number, so nothing can be judged.

## 2. Audience

Pull from `icp-and-jtbd.md`. Do not re-derive it here; do narrow it.

- **Segment:**
- **The situation they are in when they see this:**
- **What they currently believe:**
- **What they must believe after seeing it:**
- **Explicitly excluded from targeting:**
- **Estimated reachable size:**

The gap between "currently believe" and "must believe" is the actual job of the campaign. If those two lines are the same, there is no campaign.

---

## 3. Insight

One non-obvious true thing about this audience, ideally from an interview or a support ticket, that the campaign is built on. It should make someone say "huh."

```
Insight:
Source of the insight:
```

**Filled example:**

```
Insight: Trades owners do not avoid bookkeeping because it is hard. They avoid
it because opening the folder means confronting how much of last quarter they
cannot account for, and they would rather not know. Three of five interviewees
described a feeling of dread, not of difficulty.
Source: interviews 2, 3 and 5, plus 14 support tickets tagged "catch-up".
```

Note what this unlocks: the campaign should promise relief from dread and explicitly forgive the mess, not promise "easy accounting." A whole category of competitor messaging is now wrong for this audience.

**A failing insight:** "Our audience is busy and values convenience." True of everyone, so it directs nothing.

---

## 4. Single-minded proposition

One sentence. The one thing you would say if you could say only one thing. Everything in the campaign supports it or is cut.

```
SMP:
```

**Filled example:** "Hand over the mess exactly as it is — we have seen worse and we do not care."

**Test it:**

- [ ] Is it one idea, not two joined with "and"?
- [ ] Could a competitor say it? (If yes, it is not yours.)
- [ ] Does it follow from the insight?
- [ ] Would the audience find it surprising or relieving?

---

## 5. Offer

The offer is the thing the person gets and what they must do to get it. **The offer beats the ad**: a mediocre ad for a strong offer outperforms a brilliant ad for a weak one, reliably.

| Field | Instruction | Your answer |
|-------|-------------|-------------|
| What they get | Concrete, nameable | |
| What they give | Money, an email, 20 minutes, a phone number | |
| Why now | Deadline, seasonality, scarcity — must be real | |
| Risk reversal | Guarantee, free first month, no card, cancel anytime | |
| Price / cost to them | | |
| What makes this better than our standing offer | | |

**Filled example:**

| Field | Answer |
|-------|--------|
| What they get | A free "catch-up assessment": we look at last quarter's mess and tell you exactly what is missing and what it will cost to fix. Written, one page, yours to keep even if you never hire us. |
| What they give | 20 minutes and read access to their bank export |
| Why now | The Q4 filing deadline is 20 January; after 10 January we cannot take catch-up work |
| Risk reversal | No card, no call required to receive the document, no follow-up if they say no |
| Price | Free |
| Better than standing offer | The standing offer is "book a demo," which requires them to already have decided. This one gets value into their hands before any decision. |

At least four genuinely different angles, not four rewrites of one. In an AI-targeting era the creative *is* the targeting: the ad decides who leans in, more than the audience settings do.

**Filled example:**

| # | Angle | Hook line | Format | Force |
|---|-------|-----------|--------|-------|
| 1 | Absolution | "We have seen a shoebox of receipts and a bank app. We were not shocked." | Static, plain text on brand colour | Anxiety |

| 5 | Demonstration | The actual one-page summary, on screen, 15 seconds | Screen recording | Pull |

## 11. Success criteria — written BEFORE launch

Fill this in before a single euro is spent. Signed by whoever holds the budget.

| Metric | Definition | Target | Source |
|--------|-----------|--------|--------|
| Primary | | | |
| Secondary | | | |
| Guardrail 1 | | | |
| Guardrail 2 | | | |

**We will call this campaign a success if:** ______________
**We will call it a failure if:** ______________
**A result between those two means:** ______________

**Filled example:**

- Success: 60+ assessments delivered at ≤45 EUR each, and ≥20% of them convert to a paid client within 60 days.
- Failure: fewer than 25 assessments, or cost per assessment above 90 EUR, or assessment-to-client conversion below 8%.
- In between: extend 30 days at the same budget with the two best angles only, then decide.

Writing the failure line before launch is the entire point. Afterwards, everyone becomes a genius at explaining why the number they got was the number they wanted.

## 12. Kill criteria

Conditions under which the campaign stops early, agreed in advance, checked on a schedule.

| Condition | Threshold | Check when | Action |
|-----------|-----------|------------|--------|
| Cost per result far above ceiling | | | |
| No conversions after N clicks/spend | | | |
| Guardrail breached | | | |
| Qualitative | | | |

**Filled example:**

| Condition | Threshold | Check when | Action |
|-----------|-----------|------------|--------|
| Cost per assessment | > 130 EUR | Day 10, after do-not-touch window | Pause, rebuild landing page before relaunching |
| Zero conversions | 0 after 400 clicks | Continuous | Pause that ad set; the issue is the page or the offer, not the bid |
| Lead quality | > 50% outside ICP | Day 14 | Tighten offer wording, not targeting |
| Brand risk | Any comment thread turning hostile | Continuous | Owner reviews within 24h |

Note the "check when" column. A kill criterion with no scheduled check is a wish.

---

## What good looks like

The insight came from a real customer and would surprise a competitor. The single-minded proposition is one idea that only you could credibly say. The success and failure numbers were written before launch and the person who owns the budget agreed to them in writing. Creative angles address push and anxiety, not just pull. There is money left over to scale the winner.

## Common failure

Defining success after the results arrive. Every campaign produces some metric that went up, and a brief without pre-committed criteria will always be reported as a partial success, which means you learn nothing and repeat it. The second failure is a brief with five objectives — it produces creative that says everything, which reads as saying nothing.
